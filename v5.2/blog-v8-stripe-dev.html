§1Headline findings

  1. One generator draws every mountain. The hero’s massif and the four client summits come from a single landform recipe, so a change shows up everywhere at once.
  2. Contours beat renders for this job. Each line is a single height, and their spacing reads as slope, which gives a flat screen a believable form.
  3. A rounded triangle wave walks; a sine wave squiggles. Adding a low-frequency wobble stops the trail reading as drawn.
  4. Phones get a film of the same scene. It looks identical, loads in a fraction of the time and leaves the battery alone.
  5. Most good decisions were removals: fewer contours, a single accent colour, one trail instead of five.

§2Background

Tibba means a small hill. We have always described our work as climbing with a client: base camp, the ascent, the summit, and then the next peak. A mountain was the obvious symbol, which is exactly why it was hard. Obvious symbols go generic fast.

We set one constraint: no stock peaks, no low-poly triangles, no snowy photograph. The mountain had to be drawn in the studio’s language, and move as if it were being surveyed rather than rendered. In short, a map of a mountain, that you could walk.

§3Methodology

3.1 Landform. A landform is a recipe, not a model: a handful of parts summed together, each with a height, a spread and a sharpness. One part alone is a smooth cone; the character comes from what is cut into it (Table 1).

3.2 Contours. Heights are sampled on a grid and passed through marching squares: for each cell, the corners above a given height decide which short segment crosses it. Stitched together, the segments form one contour; repeated per height, they form the mountain.

// one contour at height h, over a grid of samples
for (let y = 0; y < rows; y++) for (let x = 0; x < cols; x++) {
  const c = (z[y][x] > h) | (z[y][x+1] > h) << 1
          | (z[y+1][x+1] > h) << 2 | (z[y+1][x] > h) << 3;
  if (c && c !== 15) segments.push(...edge(c, x, y, h));
}

3.3 Rendering. On screen each line is a thin ribbon of triangles drawn by a shader, so its width can follow the slope and fade with distance on the GPU. The scene has two states: lit in the loader, like a relief map, and ink as you scroll, flat lines on black that sit behind text. Aerial fade pales distant lines; a few pixels of pointer parallax give depth.1

3.4 Trail. The route is written around the summit: distance from the top shrinks steadily while the angle swings across the face. Three swing curves were compared (Table 2).

3.5 Summits. The four client summits are part of the hero’s heightfield, so the home mountain and the Our Work section read as one range from two places (Table 3).

3.6 Devices. A live WebGL scene is a lot to ask of a phone on a train, so delivery differs by device (Table 4).

§5Conclusion

Build the system, not the picture. Because every mountain comes from one generator, a change to the landform shows up in the hero, the summits, the thumbnails and the share card at once. The hours spent on the generator paid for every illustration we didn’t have to draw.

And restraint is a feature. A mountain only needs one path to the top.
