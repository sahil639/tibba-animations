What we do

An MVP has one job: prove that people want what you are building, before the money runs out. We design first versions that are small enough to ship in weeks and solid enough to put in front of real users and investors.

We start with the bet, not the screens. What problem, for whom, and what is the least you can launch to learn whether you are right? Then we design that version end to end, test it with real users, and hand your developers a product they can build without guessing.

How an MVP engagement runs

  1. Frame the bet

    01

    Product strategy with the founders: the user, the problem, the one thing the first version must do well, and what it can leave out.

  2. Talk to real users

    02

    Short, focused research before anything is drawn, so the first version answers questions people actually have.

  3. Design in sprints

    03

    Flows, UX and UI designed in weekly sprints, each ending in something you can click through and react to.

  4. Foundations from day one

    04

    A lean design system under the MVP, so version two does not mean starting again.

  5. Test and hand over

    05

    Usability tests on the prototype, fixes, and production-ready designs your developers can ship — plus the brand and launch site if you need them.

Questions

How long does it take to design an MVP?

Most MVP engagements take 4–8 weeks, depending on how much of the product exists and how much research it needs. Breathe ESG’s platform redesign took eight weeks.

Do you build the product too?

We design it and hand over production-ready UI your developers can ship. We work alongside your engineers through the build, so what launches is what was designed.

Does an MVP need a design system?

A small one, yes. A few well-made components and rules cost little at the start and save weeks when you build version two.

We already have a designer. Can you work with them?

Yes. We often work as a support function to an in-house design team, taking on a keystone project or a sprint while they keep the rest moving.

What happens after launch?

Once you have traction, the work turns to growth: onboarding, pricing and funnels that convert, and a design system that lets every team ship faster.
